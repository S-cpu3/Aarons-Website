About Me

I'm a cybersecurity researcher and graduate student at the New York Institute of Technology, pursuing a Master's in Cybersecurity alongside my Bachelor's in Computer Science. My work focuses on protecting vulnerable populations from digital surveillance threats, particularly stalkerware and privacy-invasive technologies.

As Lead Stalkerware Researcher at Operation Safe Escape, I've helped identify stalkerware on over 25 test devices within the first two months of implementation, directly supporting domestic abuse survivors in achieving safe exits. My research bridges the gap between security engineering and human rights advocacy, exploring how cryptography and detection tools can advance digital freedom.

I'm passionate about responsible disclosure, privacy rights, and developing practical security solutions for those who need them most.

Research Focus

Stalkerware Detection

Developing and evaluating tools to detect surveillance malware on mobile devices, particularly for abuse survivors. Integrated SpyGuard project and conducted field testing to improve detection accuracy and investigation speed.

Privacy & Surveillance

Investigating privacy threats posed by family surveillance apps, location tracking services, and dating app data collection. Contributing to policy research and op-eds on digital surveillance issues in urban communities.

Cellular Network Security

Analyzing cellular-level surveillance and network attacks, including 2G downgrade exploits and tower-based spying techniques. Researching open-source detection methods for mobile network threats.

Cryptography & Rights

Exploring how encryption and security technologies can advance women's rights and protect marginalized communities from state and private surveillance.
